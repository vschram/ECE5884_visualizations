Week 11 · MIMO: MRT, Spatial Multiplexing, Linear Detection, and Eigenbeamforming

Follow the Week 11 slide sequence: co-phase the transmit antennas with MRT when the transmitter knows the channel, send several streams in the same band with spatial multiplexing, separate them at the receiver with ZF and MMSE detection, and, with channel knowledge at the transmitter, diagonalize the channel by SVD and send only as many streams as the singular values support.

Transmit beamforming with CSIT, MRT, limited feedback, and TDD reciprocity

With the channel known at the transmitter, f = h*/‖h‖ co-phases every antenna at the receiver, giving SNR GEx‖h‖²/N0 and diversity order Nt

The same symbol s leaves every antenna weighted by fm, so the receiver sees one scalar channel hTf. For unit-norm f, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds |hTf|² by ‖h‖², with equality only for f ∝ h*. Reaching that bound needs channel state information at the transmitter (CSIT). The Week 10 Alamouti code needs no CSIT and pays 3 dB for it. In practice the CSIT comes either from a few feedback bits that index a codebook shared by both ends or, in TDD, from uplink sounding and reciprocity.

        V-BLAST, the MIMO signal model, channel rank, and multiplexing gain

        Nt antennas send Nt different symbols in the same band; every receive antenna hears all of them, and the singular values of H decide how many can be separated

        A 1:Nt serial-to-parallel converter gives each antenna its own stream, scaled by √(Ex/Nt) so the total transmit energy stays Ex. The receiver sees y = √(GEx/Nt) H s + v, where hi,m is the gain from transmit antenna m to receive antenna i. The channel mixes the streams (inter-stream interference). With H = UΣVH, at most rank(H) ≤ min(Nt, Nr) streams can be recovered, and the condition number σ1/σmin measures how hard that is. The reward is a rate that grows by min(Nt, Nr) bit/s/Hz for every 3 dB.

                            Linear precoding, SVD eigenbeamforming, rank adaptation, and diversity

                            With CSIT, F = Vs and the combiner UsH turn H into Ns parallel scalar channels with gains σj, and the transmitter chooses Ns from the singular values

                            A precoder F ∈ CNt×Ns maps Ns streams onto the Nt antennas, each carrying Ex/Ns. Choosing the right singular vectors, F = Vs, gives HF = UsΣs. The receiver projects onto UsH, which leaves the noise white and the streams decoupled: γj = GExσj²/(NsN0) exactly. Weak modes waste power, so rank adaptation drops them. Ns = 1 is MRT, with diversity order NtNr. Full rank gives the most streams at diversity order |Nt − Nr| + 1.

                                    Review questions from the deck
                                    1. Derive fMRT from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

                                    |hTf|² = |(h*)Hf|² ≤ ‖h‖²‖f‖² = ‖h‖², with equality if and only if f ∝ h*. With ‖f‖ = 1, fMRT = h*/‖h‖ and SNR = GEx‖h‖²/N0. On Tab 1 the MRT chain always ends on the dashed circle.

                                    2. Why does equal-weight repetition from all transmit antennas give no transmit diversity, and how do MRT and Alamouti avoid this?

                                    The receiver sees heff = Σhm/√Nt, which for i.i.d. CN(0,1) channels is again CN(0,1): a single Rayleigh channel, of order 1, and the paths can cancel. MRT uses CSIT to co-phase the paths; Alamouti spreads the symbols orthogonally over two slots so that the receiver collects ‖h‖². Compare the grey and green curves of Tab 1.

                                    3. For 2×2 ZF, compute [(HHH)−1]jj for H1 and H2.

                                    H1HH1 = [1.25 1; 1 1.25], determinant 0.5625, diagonal of the inverse 1.25/0.5625 = 2.22. H2HH2 = [1.81 1.8; 1.8 1.81], determinant 0.0361, diagonal 50.1. At GEx/N0 = 10 dB this gives 3.5 dB and −10.0 dB per stream. Use the exercise presets on Tab 3.

                                    4. Explain noise enhancement and how MMSE mitigates it.

                                    ZF leaves noise covariance N0(HHH)−1, whose diagonal grows like 1/σmin² when the columns of H are nearly parallel. MMSE inverts HHH + λI instead, which caps the gain on weak modes and trades a little residual interference for much less noise. The gap is largest at moderate SNR on ill-conditioned channels (Tab 3, third panel).

                                    5. How does eigenbeamforming decouple the MIMO streams?

                                    With F = Vs and combiner UsH, UsHHVs = Σs is diagonal, and UsHv is still white because Us has orthonormal columns. Stream j sees only σj: γj = GExσj²/(NsN0). The second panel above shows the off-diagonal entries vanishing.
