Week 10 · Diversity: Receive Combining, Transmit Diversity, and the Alamouti Code

Follow the Week 10 slide sequence: see why Rayleigh deep fades call for independent branches and separate array gain from diversity gain, combine Nr receive branches with SC, EGC and MRC, find out why repeating a symbol on several transmit antennas gives no diversity, and use the Alamouti code to obtain diversity order 2 without transmitter channel knowledge.

Motivation, diversity types, and array gain versus diversity gain

Rayleigh fading makes deep fades common, and independent branches rarely fade at the same time

With one antenna, |h|² is exponential: the SNR is more than 10 dB below its mean 10% of the time and more than 20 dB below it 1% of the time, so the average SNR overstates reliability. Diversity provides several independently fading copies of the signal. Combining them raises the mean SNR (array gain) and, more importantly, steepens the lower tail of its distribution (diversity gain). Both require branches that are sufficiently uncorrelated, which for antennas depends on their spacing.

        SIMO receive diversity: selection, equal-gain and maximal-ratio combining

        SC keeps the strongest branch, EGC co-phases every branch, and MRC weights each by its channel, so that γMRC = Σγi

                    MISO transmit diversity: repetition, delay diversity, and MRT

                    Sending the same symbol from every antenna gives one effective channel and no diversity; delay diversity and MRT avoid this in different ways

                    With Mt transmit antennas and one receive antenna, the signals add in the channel before the receiver sees them. Without channel knowledge at the transmitter (CSIT), equal-power repetition gives heff = Σhk/√Mt, which for i.i.d. branches is again CN(0,1). Delaying the copies turns the sum into a multi-tap SISO channel, whose diversity an equalizer or coded OFDM can collect. With CSIT, maximum-ratio transmission f = h*/‖h‖ aligns the paths at the receiver.

                              The Alamouti space-time code

                              A conjugate and a sign change over two slots make the channel matrix orthogonal: diversity order 2, linear detection, no CSIT, and 3 dB below MRT

                              The two antennas send (s[0], s[1])/√2 and then (−s*[1], s*[0])/√2 while h1 and h2 stay constant. Stacking y[0] and y*[1] gives ỹ = √(G/2) H s + ṽ with HHH = (|h1|² + |h2|²) I, so the matched filter HH separates the two symbols exactly, and each sees the SNR GExα/(2N0).                                       Review questions from the deck
                                      1. Define array gain and diversity order. How many branches give order 4 with MRC?

                                      Array gain is the increase in average SNR, Ag = γ̄Σ/γ̄. Diversity order M is the high-SNR slope, P̄s ≈ cγ̄−M: M decades per 10 dB. MRC over Nr i.i.d. branches has order Nr, so four independent branches are needed. Check it on the slope panel of Tab 2.

                                      2. Derive the MRC weights for y = √G h s + v.

                                      The SNR of z = wHy is |wHh|²GEx/(‖w‖²N0). By Cauchy–Schwarz, |wHh|² ≤ ‖w‖²‖h‖², with equality for w ∝ h. Hence γMRC = GEx‖h‖²/N0 = Σγi: the dashed circle on the phasor panel of Tab 2.

                                      3. Why does sending s/√2 on both transmit antennas fail?

                                      The receiver sees heff = (h1 + h2)/√2. For i.i.d. CN(0,1) channels, heff is again CN(0,1), exactly a SISO channel, so the order stays 1, and the two paths can cancel. See the CDF panel of Tab 3.

                                      4. Show that HHH is diagonal for the Alamouti code.

                                      With H = [h1 h2; h2* −h1*], the off-diagonal entry is h1*h2 + h2(−h1*) = 0, and both diagonal entries are |h1|² + |h2|². Remove the sign change above: the off-diagonal entry becomes 2h1*h2.

                                      5. Explain the 3 dB gap between Alamouti and MRT.

                                      Both depend on ‖h‖², so both have order 2. Without CSIT, Alamouti splits Ex equally over the antennas and cannot steer it, giving GExα/(2N0). MRT phase-aligns the paths with CSIT and gets GExα/N0, twice as much at every block (the parallel curves on the left).

                                      Receive combiningSC: γ̄SC = γ̄Σ1/k. MRC: w = h, γ = Σγi. Both reach order Nr.
                                      Blind repetitionheff = Σhk/√Mt ~ CN(0,1): order 1, and the paths can cancel. Never the right choice.
                                      Alamouti (no CSIT)HHH = αI, γ = GExα/(2N0). Order 2, rate 1 for Mt = 2, linear detection.
