                          One-tap noise calculation

                          Dividing by ĥ restores the desired symbol scale, but it applies the same gain to AWGN. The equalizer cannot recover SNR lost in the channel fade.

                          Equalizer noise-gain calculation

                          The equalizer is a filter, so white noise passes through it filtered too. For white v[n] the output noise power is scaled by the energy of the tap vector, ‖f‖², whatever the taps do to the signal. Dividing by the main cursor c[nd] to restore the symbol scale divides the signal and the noise alike, so the output noise power relative to the restored symbol is ‖f‖² / |c[nd]|². Above 1 the equalizer enhances noise; below 1 it attenuates, which a channel with gain above unity allows.

                          Worst zero-forcing gain

                          A zero-forcing equalizer asks for 1/H at every frequency, so its largest gain sits exactly where the channel is weakest. The gain multiplies the noise in that band by the same factor, and its square multiplies the noise power. The curve on screen is clamped at |H| = 0.02, which caps the plotted gain at 50× for the deepest notch the slider allows.

                          Worst LMMSE gain

                          The LMMSE weight |H|/(|H|²+λ) cannot exceed 1/(2√λ) whatever the channel does, because the λ in the denominator takes over once |H|² falls below it. That bound is what stops noise enhancement: it depends only on the noise level, not on how deep the channel's notch is.
