Multicarrier motivation

A wideband channel has memory; splitting the band into N narrow subcarriers takes it away

One wideband carrier sampled every T = 1/B seconds sees channel memory L = ⌈τmax/T⌉. Divide the same B into N subcarriers and each one is N times narrower and its useful symbol N times longer, so it sees a channel that is almost flat — one complex gain instead of a filter.

              Orthogonality, spectra, and out-of-band emissions

              Integer cycles make the subcarriers orthogonal; the transmit window shapes their sidelobes

              Subcarrier k and receiver basis m differ by an integer number of cycles over the useful block, so their inner product is exactly zero unless k = m. Their sinc-like spectra overlap, but the nulls land on the other subcarrier centres. A smoother time-domain window reduces the out-of-band sidelobes at the cost of transition time.

                                    Why SC-FDE uses N₀/Ex and OFDM uses NN₀/EX

                                    Both denominators are the same thing — the inverse per-subcarrier SNR — written for different symbol mappings. In SC-FDE, X[k] is the DFT of a block of time-domain symbols of energy Ex, so its energy picks up the same factor N that the noise does and the N cancels. In OFDM, Xk is the transmitted symbol on subcarrier k with energy EX, while the noise still gains N from the non-unitary DFT, so the factor survives. With a unitary DFT it disappears from both.
