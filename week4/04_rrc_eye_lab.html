How this BPSK eye diagram is constructed

This laboratory transmits BPSK symbols ak ∈ {−1,+1}. After pulse shaping, the channel, and optional receive filtering, one long received waveform is divided into two-symbol windows. Consecutive windows begin one symbol period apart and therefore overlap by T. Only middle, steady-state windows are used, so filter startup and shutdown transients are excluded.

long received waveform → cut into overlapping 2T windows → align each window to 0…2T → overlay

The three coloured span bars mark consecutive windows explicitly: W1=[0,2T], W2=[T,3T], and W3=[2T,4T]. Their vertical separation keeps the overlapping portions visible. The final stage adds the other steady-state windows faintly; this is the eye diagram above. The red line marks the receiver sampling instant T+τ.

What would change for QPSK? Construct the same eye separately for the in-phase and quadrature BPSK-like components. With Es=1, their ideal levels are ±1/√2 rather than ±1. Under identical I/Q filtering the two eyes have the same shape; I/Q imbalance or unequal channel distortion would make them differ.
