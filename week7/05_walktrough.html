Week 7 · Frequency-Domain Equalization (SC-FDE and OFDM)

Follow one block of symbols from a channel too long for time-domain equalization, through the cyclic prefix that makes the block circular, to per-subcarrier equalization in SC-FDE and OFDM.

Why move to the frequency domain?

Channel memory grows with symbol rate; time-domain equalizer cost grows with it

For a fixed maximum excess delay τmax, the discrete channel memory is L ≈ ⌈Rsτmax⌉. Filtering costs O(Lf) per symbol and a dense design costs O(Lf³). Block FFT processing costs O(N log N) per block and stops tracking L once N is fixed.

                                                        CP length Lc ≥ LSets whether the per-subcarrier model holds at all. Too short leaves inter-block interference no equalizer can undo.
                                                        Block size NLarger N amortizes the CP but adds latency and assumes the channel is static over N + Lc symbols.
                                                        Deep fadesZF inverts them and amplifies noise. MMSE, coding across subcarriers, and frequency diversity are the standard answers.
                                                        PAPROFDM superposes N subcarriers and needs power-amplifier back-off; SC-FDE and DFT-spread OFDM keep a single-carrier envelope.
                                                        Carrier frequency offsetResidual CFO destroys subcarrier orthogonality in OFDM and needs extra synchronization (Heath Sec. 5.4.4).

                                                        Worst zero-forcing gain

                                                        Zero forcing asks for 1/H[k] in every bin, so its largest gain sits exactly where the channel is weakest. That gain multiplies the noise in that bin by the same factor, and its square multiplies the noise power. Nothing downstream can recover the SNR lost in the fade.

                                                        Worst MMSE gain

                                                        The MMSE weight |H|/(|H|²+λ) is maximized at |H| = √λ, where it equals 1/(2√λ). Whatever the channel does, the λ in the denominator takes over once |H|² falls below it, so the gain is bounded by the noise level rather than by the depth of the null. The price is that weak bins are left partially unequalized.

                                                        Why SC-FDE uses N₀/Ex and OFDM uses NN₀/EX

                                                        Both denominators are the same thing — the inverse per-subcarrier SNR — written for different symbol mappings. In SC-FDE, X[k] is the DFT of a block of time-domain symbols of energy Ex, so its energy picks up the same factor N that the noise does and the N cancels. In OFDM, Xk is the transmitted symbol on subcarrier k with energy EX, while the noise still gains N from the non-unitary DFT, so the factor survives. With a unitary DFT it disappears from both.
